# Tabula Latina

A personal Latin trainer for the YouTube course
[Easy Latin Lessons](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLjqqMkeHFt1E_lRi9nQvtmP9Od2JokI1J),
published as a private claude.ai artifact.

- **Four tabs, one button**: Domus (today: one big Continue that always does
  the right next thing, the day's goal, and Answer in Latin, the next story and
  your messages), Lectiōnēs (your whole path: lessons, rules, stories, Latin in
  English, the endings), Verba (your words and the mystery cards) and Nūntiī
  (the messenger).
- **Rules one at a time**: each video's rules in small parts (22 for Lessons
  I-IV). A part is explained very simply with examples, then two open questions
  are answered in your own words (checked by their key words, by Claude, or
  against a good answer). Only then do its exercises open; a rule you have not
  learned never appears in practice. Ten right answers make a rule learned.
- **Practice**: every word you know, only the rules you have learned, in short
  blocks of one kind of exercise from easy to hard (choose the word, pick the
  meaning, build from tiles, type an ending in the gap; then whole sentences to
  write and change), with the day's mistakes and due words in between. Feedback
  names the case or person you used and the one needed, plus the rule behind it;
  one Help button shows the rule, a hint and the colors. Only sensible sentences.
- **Words**: your dictionary with spaced repetition; each word has its forms,
  sensible example sentences and, where one exists, a famous Latin saying. Five
  mystery cards lie on top, each with a Latin word related to English, Russian
  or Ukrainian words; opened cards turn face up where they lie; add them and
  they join the exercises.
- **Audio**: every Latin sentence can be heard in classical pronunciation
  (through the device's Spanish or Italian voice), Listen & repeat on lesson pages, and a
  Listen and write exercise.
- **Sentences from the videos**: the sentences you heard, as exercises, from
  each lesson's page: choose the form, pick the meaning, build them from word
  tiles, write them.
- **Fābulae (short stories)**: twelve stories, three for each lesson, made only
  of the words from the videos; each opens when you have learned its rules. Tap a line for its
  English and a word for its meaning, listen, answer two questions, then practise
  the story's lines.
- **Answer in Latin**: questions in Latin ("Quid legis?", quid = what) about you,
  your friends, the stories you read and little imagined scenes, fresh from Claude
  each round and answered with your own words, as creatively as you like; the
  site checks the grammar and the coach judges the answer.
- **Nūntiī (messages)**: a messenger with five friends in and around Rome
  around 100 AD (Iūlia, Marcus, Gāius, Claudia, Lūcius), their group chat, and
  "Someone new": a stranger from anywhere in the Roman world, rolled at random
  (from ordinary to extreme or odd) and made real by Claude. They write only with
  your words and the rules you have learned, have Roman portraits, their own
  hours, moods and happenings that change at random, text first now and then,
  type, read and answer when awake; a scheduled routine lets them write while
  the page is closed and notifies your phone. Tap a message for its English, a
  word for what it is; Check asks the coach about your Latin, and you can ask
  the coach anything about it right there. They live through the Roman year in
  real time (2026 is AD 100, under Trajan): festivals, birthdays and the news of
  the day come up in their talk. Now and then they send a voice message (played
  in their own voice, classical Latin) or a photo from their world (Roman
  paintings and things from The Met). They know each other and most strangers
  know one of them, and they take your mistakes literally («Aqua tē bibit?!»).
  Hand-drawn Roman emoji (SPQR, the
  she-wolf, the legion's eagle, a laurel wreath…) sit beside the usual ones. It looks like an
  old Roman letter desk: parchment panels among real engravings (Piranesi's
  Forum, Corinthian columns, Livy's bust), laurels and big faces.
- **Your own words**: add any Latin word (or look one up by its English,
  Russian or Ukrainian meaning) from the Words page, Ask Claude or the
  messenger; Claude fills in its card (forms, origin, history, relatives,
  examples), the grammar engine checks every form, and the word joins your
  exercises once its endings are learned.
- **Word stories**: ask Claude about a word and you also hear where it comes
  from, a bit of its history and a hidden link you might have missed (euge →
  Eugene → eugenics); send just a word for its word card.
- **Claude settings**: the model (Haiku, Sonnet, Opus) and how hard it thinks,
  chosen separately for the coach and for the people in the messenger.
- **Mistakes come back**: your mistakes return later as new exercises on the
  same rules and words, between the exercises of your next rounds.
- **Latin in English**: what English took from Latin, from Roman traders to
  modern science; the English words that grew from each word you know; Latin
  verb forms, plurals, grammar rules and abbreviations inside English. Read one
  short screen at a time, with a quick question now and then.
- **Ask Claude**: a tutor that sees your lessons, words and weak spots, and what
  is on your screen (the exercise, the lines of a lesson in view, the word or card
  you opened, the chat you are in), so you can ask "what does this sentence mean?".

Portraits: painted mummy portraits from Roman Egypt and a Boscoreale wall
painting, from The Met, the Cleveland Museum of Art and the Badisches
Landesmuseum Karlsruhe (CC0) and the Medelhavsmuseet, Stockholm (CC BY 4.0);
each profile credits its own.
